Case 82
You are given two recommendations. Your task is to evaluate their similarity by scoring each of the following criteria: completeness, step-by-step concordance, case-tailoring concordance, and missing data concordance. To perform your evaluation, follow the sequence of actions indicated below. 
Action 1 - PRE-SCORING DECISION EXTRACTION
Before assigning any score, identify the key clinical decisions contained in both recommendations. For each recommendation, summarize the following elements: 
- Overall oncological strategy
- Systemic therapy strategy
- Surgical strategy
- Timing of surgery
- Key staging/restaging investigations
- Anatomical compliance assessment
- Critical missing data
- Final MDT decision
Important: The purpose of this step is to identify the underlying clinical decisions. Thus, 
- Do not evaluate wording, style, length, formatting, or level of detail.
- Only extract the clinical meaning of each recommendation.
The subsequent scoring should be based primarily on the extracted decisions rather than on the original text.

Action 2 - EVALUATION CRITERIA
Criterion 1 — Clinical Coverage
Evaluate whether Recommendation 2 addresses the same major clinical domains as Recommendation 1.
Examples include:
- resectability assessment
- systemic therapy
- imaging and staging
- surgical management
- postoperative management
- surveillance strategy
Scoring:
5 = Same clinically relevant domains covered
4 = Minor omissions with no impact on management
3 = Some relevant domains missing
2 = Major clinical domains missing
1 = Multiple critical domains absent

Criterion 2 — Step-By-Step Concordance
Evaluate whether Recommendation 2 reproduces the same major clinical decisions and management sequence as Recommendation 1.
Focus on:
Step 1
- overall treatment strategy
- timing of surgery
- systemic therapy decisions
- staging/restaging decisions
- treatment sequencing
Step 3: anatomical compliance assessment
Scoring:
5 = Same management strategy and same resectability conclusions
4 = Same overall strategy with minor differences
3 = Partially overlapping strategy
2 = Important strategic disagreements
1 = Opposite management strategy or opposite resectability assessment
Minor differences in wording, sequencing, or level of detail should not reduce the score.

Criterion 3 — Case-Tailoring Concordance
Evaluate whether Recommendation 2 incorporates the same case-specific clinical factors used by Recommendation 1. Focus on whether similar patient-specific reasoning is applied.
Examples include:
- molecular profile
- tumor biology
- chemotherapy response
- lesion distribution
- vascular involvement
- extrahepatic disease
- patient-related factors
- timing of metastatic presentation
Scoring:
5 = Same case-specific reasoning
4 = Minor differences
3 = Partial overlap
2 = Important patient-specific factors ignored
1 = Major case-specific reasoning absent

Criterion 4 — Missing Data Concordance
Determine whether Recommendation 2 identifies the same decision-critical missing information as Recommendation 1.
Instructions:
- Evaluate Step 2 only.
- Assess the overlap in missing data identified across the different categories (A, B, C, D).
- Focus exclusively on information that could realistically alter treatment decisions.
- Do not penalize Recommendation 2 for omitting desirable but non-essential information.
Scoring:
5 = Same critical missing data identified
4 = Minor differences
3 = Partial overlap
2 = Important discrepancies
1 = Completely different missing-data assessment

Criterion 5 — MDT DECISION CONCORDANCE
Evaluate whether Recommendation 2 would ultimately lead to the same MDT decision as Recommendation 1.
Focus on the final treatment recommendation rather than individual details.
Examples of high concordance:
- upfront surgery vs upfront surgery
- conversion chemotherapy vs conversion chemotherapy
- two-stage hepatectomy vs two-stage hepatectomy
- surgery contraindicated vs surgery contraindicated
Examples of low concordance:
- upfront surgery vs induction chemotherapy
- resectable vs unresectable
- surgery vs no surgery
Consider the final treatment pathway that would be proposed to the patient.
Scoring:
5 = Same MDT decision
- Same overall treatment pathway
- Same resectability conclusion
- Differences limited to details or supporting arguments
4 = Highly similar MDT decision
- Minor differences in sequencing
- Final treatment recommendation remains essentially unchanged
3 = Partially similar MDT decision
- Similar therapeutic goals
- Important implementation differences
2 = Different MDT decision
- Significant management differences
- Different treatment pathway likely
1 = Opposite MDT decision
- Opposite conclusions regarding surgery, resectability, or treatment intent
- Fundamentally different patient management

Action 3 - EVALUATION PROCESS
Using the clinical decisions extracted in Action 1, compare the two recommendations against each criterion, applying the definitions provided for each score value in Action 2. Support your scores with a brief reasoning for each criterion, based on the clinical decisions identified in Action 1 and the content of the two recommendations.
Important: Scores should reflect agreement in clinical reasoning and management decisions rather than textual similarity.

CLINICAL CASE
Man, 64 years old, left colon adenocarcinoma with four bilobar liver metastases. CEA 900 ng/mL, RAS wild-type. Liver MRI: segment VII lesion invading the IVC over 1 cm.

RECOMMENDATION 1 (EXPERT REFERENCE)
Step 1 – Oncological strategy
A. Primary tumor management
• Left colon adenocarcinoma with four bilobar liver metastases.
• Primary tumor resection should be coordinated after systemic therapy and liver reassessment.
• Left hemicolectomy can be performed in a staged or combined strategy if hepatic conversion becomes feasible.
B. Systemic therapy
• Indication: mandatory conversion chemotherapy.
• Regimen: intensive chemotherapy ± anti-EGFR therapy given RAS wild-type status.
• Objective: tumor downsizing, reduction of IVC invasion, and biologic assessment.
C. Reassessment
• Liver MRI and CT after 2–3 months.
• PET-CT given CEA 900.
• Evaluate response of the segment VII lesion and relationship to the IVC.
• FLR calculation mandatory.
D. Liver-directed strategy
• Surgery only if major response and acceptable vascular reconstruction strategy.
• Consider one- or two-stage hepatectomy with parenchymal-sparing resections.
• Partial IVC resection/reconstruction may be necessary.
• Intraoperative ultrasound mandatory.
• Multidisciplinary vascular surgery involvement required.
E. Postoperative strategy
• Complete approximately 6 months of systemic therapy.
• Close surveillance with imaging and CEA.
Step 2 – Missing data
1.	Patient Assessment: Patient’s current performance status and comorbidities. Patient’s surgical fitness for major procedures. Treatment tolerance
2.	Primary Tumor Status: MSI/MSS status and differentiation.
3.	Liver Metastatic Disease Assessment: Exact extent/depth of IVC invasion.
Full segmental distribution of all lesions. FLR estimation.
4.	Extrahepatic Metastatic Disease Assessment: Chest CT scan.

Step 3 – Resectability assessment
Anatomical Compliance
• Four bilobar lesions.
• Segment VII lesion invading the IVC.
• Complex vascular surgery required for R0 resection.
→ Conclusion: Anatomically borderline/non-compliant upfront.
Potentially convertible after downsizing.

RECOMMENDATION 2 (CANDIDATE)

Step 1 – Oncological strategy  
- Initiate systemic chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFOX or FOLFIRI + anti‑EGFR antibody given RAS wild‑type) as a conversion/biologic test.  
- Re‑image after 2–3 months (contrast MRI or CT) to assess tumor response, especially the IVC‑invasive segment VII lesion and the other three metastases.  
- If disease is stable or responding and no new extra‑hepatic sites appear, proceed with a liver‑first (hepatic‑first) approach because the primary left‑sided colon tumor is presumed asymptomatic.  
- Plan a staged hepatic resection:  
   • If the IVC involvement can be resected with negative margins, schedule en bloc segment VII resection with IVC segmental resection and prosthetic or autologous reconstruction, combined with parenchyma‑sparing resections of the remaining lesions.  
   • If the IVC involvement is deemed unresectable, consider alternative local therapies (e.g., stereotactic body radiotherapy or percutaneous ablation) for that lesion while resecting the other metastases.  
- After hepatic surgery, reassess the colon primary; perform definitive colectomy (open or minimally invasive) once the patient has recovered, unless the primary becomes symptomatic earlier.  
- Continue adjuvant systemic therapy (total 6 months of peri‑operative chemotherapy) tailored to response and tolerability.  

Step 2 – Essential missing data for each category  
A) Patient Assessment  
- Current performance status (ECOG or Karnofsky) / ASA classification.  
- Cardiopulmonary comorbidities (e.g., cardiac ejection fraction, pulmonary function).  
- Baseline liver function (bilirubin, INR, albumin, platelet count) and presence of chemotherapy‑associated liver injury.  
- Future liver remnant (FLR) volumetry and calculation of FLR % of total liver volume.  

B) Primary Tumor Status  
- Clinical T and N stage of the left‑sided colon cancer.  
- Presence of symptoms (obstruction, bleeding, perforation) that would mandate urgent primary resection.  
- Status of the primary on colonoscopy (extent, feasibility of endoscopic stenting if needed).  

C) Liver Metastatic Disease Assessment  
- Precise dimensions and spatial relationship of the IVC‑invasive lesion (length of IVC involvement, proximity to hepatic veins).  
- Vascular anatomy of hepatic veins and portal branches relative to all four metastases.  
- Whether any additional occult lesions are present (intra‑operative ultrasound findings).  

D) Extra‑hepatic disease  
- Staging of the chest (CT) to rule out pulmonary metastases.  
- Evaluation for peritoneal or distant nodal disease (CT or PET‑CT).  

These items are required for a definitive operative plan, but a reasonable MDT recommendation can already be made based on the available information.  

Step 3 – Anatomically compliant, provided that:  
- Adequate FLR (≥30 % of functional liver in the setting of prior chemotherapy, or ≥20 % if liver parenchyma is pristine) can be preserved.  
- The IVC segment involved can be resected with negative margins and safely reconstructed (either with a patch, interposition graft, or primary repair).  
- No occult extra‑hepatic disease is identified on staging imaging.  

Rationale: The presence of IVC invasion is a technical challenge but not an absolute contraindication; with appropriate vascular expertise and sufficient liver remnant, resection is feasible. Missing data listed above must be confirmed to ensure patient safety and surgical feasibility.
