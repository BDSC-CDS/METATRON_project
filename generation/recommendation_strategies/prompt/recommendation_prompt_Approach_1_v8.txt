RECOMMENDATION STRUCTURE
The recommendation must be structured in three steps:

Step 1 - Oncological strategy
Recommend the most appropriate oncological strategy or sequence of strategies, including surgery. Provide a detailed, step-by-step approach tailored to the specific clinical presentation.

Step 2 - Missing data
Determine whether any missing information prevents a reliable treatment recommendation.
Classify missing information into three categories:
A. Patient Assessment: Information related to the patient’s fitness for major liver surgery.
B. Primary Tumor Status: Information related to the control and oncological status of the primary colorectal cancer (symptomatic or not).
C. Liver Metastatic Disease Assessment: Information related to the extent, distribution, and technical resectability of liver metastases.

For each category:
•	List only data that are truly required to make a treatment recommendation.
•	Do not list information that would merely increase confidence.
•	Assume that MDT decisions are routinely made despite incomplete datasets.

Procedure to identify only essential missing data (i.e., without which a reliable decision cannot be made): before listing missing information, answer the following question: "Can a reasonable MDT recommendation already be formulated based on the available information?” Only if the answer is NO, specify which items prevent a reliable recommendation.

Step 3 - Anatomic resectability
Determine whether any anatomical barrier to surgical resection can be identified from the available information and classify the case into one of two categories:
•	Compliant: No anatomical barrier is identified, or any concern is solely due to missing data rather than a confirmed barrier.
•	Non-compliant: A definite anatomical barrier precludes safe resection.
If the case is classified as Compliant but critical anatomical data are missing, respond with: "Compliant, provided that: [list the missing data required to confirm feasibility]". 
Do not classify a case as Non-compliant solely on the basis of missing information.

Important rules
•	Do not assume anatomical non-compliance solely because specific anatomical data are unavailable.
•	If no anatomical barrier is described, assume anatomical compliance unless available information strongly suggests otherwise.
•	Missing volumetry, vascular mapping, biliary mapping, or liver function assessment should not automatically result in anatomical non-compliance.
