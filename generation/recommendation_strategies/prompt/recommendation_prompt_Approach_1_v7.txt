RECOMMENDATION STRUCTURE
The recommendation must be structured in three steps:

Step 1 - Oncological strategy
Recommend the most appropriate oncological strategy or sequence of strategies, including surgery. Provide a detailed, step-by-step approach tailored to the specific clinical presentation.

Step 2 - Missing data

Step 3 - Resectability assessment
Provide a resectability assessment using two independent compliance evaluations:

3a - Oncological Status Compliance
Evaluate whether the current oncological status permits consideration of resection:
1. Primary tumor status (resected R0 or resectable? any uncontrolled symptoms?)
2. Extrahepatic disease (absent, or present but completely resectable/ablatable?)
3. Oncological response to systemic therapy (stable/responding vs progressing on adequate regimen)
4. Tumor biology surrogates (RAS/BRAF/MSI, CEA trend, resection-free interval)
5. Timing of presentation (synchronous vs metachronous)

Classify as:
- Oncologically compliant: The oncological situation meets conditions for safe resection consideration. No oncological contraindications exist. All parameters favorable or controlled. No absolute oncological contraindication.
- Oncologically non-compliant: At least one absolute contraindication present. Specify which parameter fails and whether conversion is possible. Resection is excluded in the current setting due to oncological reasons, regardless of anatomical feasibility.

3b - Anatomical Compliance
Evaluate whether the tumor anatomy and patient factors permit safe surgical resection. Classify as:
- Anatomically compliant: Surgery is technically and functionally feasible considering FLR ratio, vascular involvement, biliary involvement and margin. No anatomical, functional, or technical barriers preclude resection.
- Anatomically non-compliant: Resection is precluded by anatomical, functional, or technical factors. Specific barriers must be explicitly named.
