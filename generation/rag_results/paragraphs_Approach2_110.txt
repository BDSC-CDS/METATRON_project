    "\nIn summary, the criteria for resectability of colorectal liver\nmetastases have been significantly revised and expanded\nover the course of the last decade. Clinicopathologic factors\nsuch as the tumor number, tumor size, presence of extrahepatic disease, or width of the resection margin should no\nlonger be used to categorically exclude patients from consideration for surgical resection. Similarly, the use of preoperative\nchemotherapy can be associated with conversion of some\npatients from unresectable to resectable. Preoperative chemotherapy may also be useful in the treatment of patients\nwith intra- and extrahepatic disease. In this setting, preoperative chemotherapy can reduce disease volume so that an\nR0 resection is more feasible, but perhaps more importantly, preoperative chemotherapy can help assess the underlying biology of the patient\u2019s disease.     "\nPreviously, clinicopathologic factors were used to define\nthe appropriateness of attempting hepatectomy for colorectal metastases. These factors concentrated on the lesions to\nbe removed and patient factors rather than the liver that\nwould remain. In a 1986 publication by Ekberg et al. [37],\nthe authors proposed several general contraindications to\nhepatectomy for metastatic colorectal cancer. These criteria\nincluded: four or more metastases within the liver, additional extrahepatic metastatic disease, large size of hepatic\nmetastases, and the inability to achieve a resection margin\nof at least 1 cm. Multiple subsequent studies from other\ngroups then corroborated some or all of these findings.\nWith time, these clinicopathologic factors were used to determine which patients with colorectal liver metastases had\n\u201cresectable\u201d disease.\nSubsequently, these studies were criticized both on clinical and methodological grounds. One major problem is\nthat only a small number of patients who actually met all the\n\u201cunresectable\u201d criteria were included in the analyses. Additionally, most of these studies used only univariate (logrank) analyses, which do not adjust for competing risk\nfactors that may have affected survival. Systemic chemotherapy and the availability of adjunctive techniques such\nas radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and portal vein embolization (PVE) have greatly changed our management of patients. These new techniques make the application of these\n\nolder data to current paradigms of treatment problematic.\nCommunity physicians, oncologists, and even some surgeons still use these factors to determine resectability of patients with colorectal metastases and fail to refer potentially\nresectable patients.\n\n",
