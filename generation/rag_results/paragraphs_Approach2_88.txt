    "\nIn summary, the criteria for resectability of colorectal liver\nmetastases have been significantly revised and expanded\nover the course of the last decade. Clinicopathologic factors\nsuch as the tumor number, tumor size, presence of extrahepatic disease, or width of the resection margin should no\nlonger be used to categorically exclude patients from consideration for surgical resection. Rather, assessment of resectability should take into account the ability to obtain an\nR0 resection of all known disease\u2014whether it be intra- or\nextrahepatic. The feasibility of hepatic resection should be\ndetermined chiefly using three criteria: (a) the ability to preserve two contiguous hepatic segments, (b) the preservation\nof vascular inflow and outflow as well as biliary drainage,\nand (c) the ability to preserve an adequate FLR. In those patients with extensive disease and limited hepatic reserve,\nPVE or a two-stage hepatectomy approach can sometimes\npermit surgical resection. Similarly, the use of preoperative\nchemotherapy can be associated with conversion of some\npatients from unresectable to resectable. Preoperative chemotherapy may also be useful in the treatment of patients\nwith intra- and extrahepatic disease. In this setting, preoperative chemotherapy can reduce disease volume so that an\nR0 resection is more feasible, but perhaps more importantly, preoperative chemotherapy can help assess the underlying biology of the patient\u2019s disease. Although the\ncriteria for resectability of colorectal liver metastases have\nbeen expanded to include patients with both intra- and extrahepatic disease, these patients must be very well selected. Patients with extensive extrahepatic disease, or\nthose who progress on systemic chemotherapy, should\nprobably not be considered for hepatic resection. The expansion of criteria for resectability of colorectal liver metastases requires a much more sophisticated approach to the\npatient with advanced disease. Patients require ongoing repeat evaluations by a surgeon with expertise in hepatic resection.
