A multicenter RCT comparing DEB-TACE with irinotecan\nbeads (DEBIRI-TACE) with systemic chemotherapy with systemic\nchemotherapy alone in unresectable CRLM found higher response\nrates, longer PFS (15.2 vs 7.6, respectively), and greater conversion to\nhepatic resection in the combination group [61]. The PARAGON II trial\nexplored the role of DEBIRI-TACE for potentially resectable CRLM. Of 40\npatients recruited in the trial who underwent preoperative DEBIRITACE, 38 (95.0%) underwent resection with an R0 resection rate of\n74.0% [62]. Nonetheless, this technique should still be considered as a\nsecondary treatment option in CRLM and more so in BR-CRLM.\n\n_Ablation techniques_\nRFA, MWA, and the relatively novel technique of irreversible\nelectroporation (IRE) are the ablation modalities tried and tested in\nCRLM. A major limitation of RFA is the heat sink effect, which occurs\nwhen a tumor abuts a blood vessel of a size of > 3 mm. This limita\u00ad\ntion is somewhat circumvented with MWA, which produces more\nheat. However, IRE is not impeded by the heat sink effect of neigh\u00ad\nboring blood vessels. The ablation of tumor size up to 3 cm has\nshown to be equally effective as resection in terms of oncologically\noutcomes [63,64]. For tumor sizes of 3 to 5 cm, ablation can be\n\nconsidered by overlapping multiple approaches to achieve complete\ntumor coverage. Ablation is generally not recommended for a tumor\nsize larger than 5 cm [65]. The role of ablation in BR-CRLM is in\npatients with bilobar lesions in which complete R0 resection cannot\nbe achieved without adequate FLR. Hepatectomy combined with\nintraoperative or postoperative ablation can achieve acceptable re\u00ad\nsults as R0 resection by preserving adequate FLR [66,67]. A study\ncomparing OSH with ablation vs TSH or hepatectomy alone in CRLM\ndid not find any difference in the 5-year OS [66]. For patients pre\u00ad\nsenting with postresection recurrence, image-guided ablation pro\u00ad\nvides an effective alternative strategy to liver resection [68]. Multiple\nsessions of ablation can be performed with less destruction of\nnormal liver parenchyma, resulting in survival similar to patients\nwithout recurrence [69]. The results of the MAVERRIC and COLLIS\u00ad\nION trials have shown that thermal ablation is an equally valid\ncurative treatment modality compared with surgery for a CRLM of\n\u22643 cm [70,71].\n\n_RT_\nLocal regional treatment with RT consists of conventional RT,\nstereotactic beam RT (SBRT), TARE, and brachytherapy. The roles of\nSBRT and TARE in patients with CRLM who are not surgical candi\u00ad\ndates (unresectable) and/or who have failed first- or second-line\nchemotherapy have been described. SBRT has a complementary role\nwith other locoregional therapies, such as ablation. A recent metaanalysis concluded that SBRT for liver oligometastases is an effective\ntreatment option for patients with advanced colorectal cancer, en\u00ad\ncouraging local control and survival [72]. However, the role of SBRT\nas a downstaging strategy to achieve resectability has not yet elu\u00ad\ncidated and currently remains only as an effective alternative\ntreatment strategy for patients who are not suitable surgical can\u00ad\ndidates. TARE using yttrium-90 (Y-90), a type of selective intra-ar\u00ad\nterial radiotherapy, is a therapeutic alternative for selected patients\nwith CRLM. As a part of their\nevidence-based expert consensus treatment algorithm, Jeyarajah\net al. [10] described the role of Y-90 TARE in BR-CRLM. TARE has a\nrole in presurgical treatment in patients with BR-CRLM who have\nbeen subjected to systemic chemotherapy but have inadequate\nradiologic response, precluding a safe resection without adequate\nFLR, or in patients who remain unresectable after systemic che\u00ad\nmotherapy [10]. Radiation segmentectomy/lobectomy is a promising\ntechnique that uses ablative technique for patients who are not\nsurgical candidates and tumors not amenable to ablation.     "\nPreviously, clinicopathologic factors were used to define\nthe appropriateness of attempting hepatectomy for colorectal metastases. These factors concentrated on the lesions to\nbe removed and patient factors rather than the liver that\nwould remain. In a 1986 publication by Ekberg et al. [37],\nthe authors proposed several general contraindications to\nhepatectomy for metastatic colorectal cancer. These criteria\nincluded: four or more metastases within the liver, additional extrahepatic metastatic disease, large size of hepatic\nmetastases, and the inability to achieve a resection margin\nof at least 1 cm. Multiple subsequent studies from other\ngroups then corroborated some or all of these findings.\nWith time, these clinicopathologic factors were used to determine which patients with colorectal liver metastases had\n\u201cresectable\u201d disease.\nSubsequently, these studies were criticized both on clinical and methodological grounds. One major problem is\nthat only a small number of patients who actually met all the\n\u201cunresectable\u201d criteria were included in the analyses. Additionally, most of these studies used only univariate (logrank) analyses, which do not adjust for competing risk\nfactors that may have affected survival. Systemic chemotherapy and the availability of adjunctive techniques such\nas radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and portal vein embolization (PVE) have greatly changed our management of patients. These new techniques make the application of these\n\nolder data to current paradigms of treatment problematic.\nCommunity physicians, oncologists, and even some surgeons still use these factors to determine resectability of patients with colorectal metastases and fail to refer potentially\nresectable patients.\n\n"
