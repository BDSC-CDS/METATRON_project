The definition of resectability remains\na challenge and is still evolving, and the reported resection rate is mainly biased toward\nhigh-volume centers. The European Colorectal Metastases Treatment Group (ECMTG)\nproposed that resection should be considered for patients with more than 30% post-surgery\neven after portal vein embolization, absence of celiac lymph node involvement or extrahepatic disease, and without invasion of two branches of liver pedicle, inferior vena cava or\nthree hepatic veins [10]. The rationale for adjuvant chemotherapy after resection of primary\ntumor and LM is based on the efficacy of chemotherapy in adjuvant setting for stage\nIII CRC and palliative settings for metastatic CRC. However, this efficacy is not directly\nrelated to adjuvant settings after resection of LM. A few clinical trials were designed to\nestablish the best treatment strategy in resectable LM; however, poor recruitment, the\nneed for relatively long duration of follow-up and the use of old drugs such as oral uraciltegafur, 5-fluorouracil/leucovorin (5-FU/LV) were limitations [11\u201314]. The FFCD ACHBTH AURC 9002 trial\ncompared surgery including resection of primary tumor and LM with surgery followed\nby 5-FU/LV chemotherapy [13]. The trial was based on 171 patients. The 5-year DFS\nrate was 33.5% in patients undergoing surgery followed by chemotherapy (5-FU/LV),\n26.7% in those treated with surgery alone ( _p_ = 0.028), and the 5-year OS rate was 51.1% vs.\n41.1% ( _p_ = 0.13). Post-operative 5-FU/LV improved DFS but not OS. Surgery followed by\n5-FU/LV and irinotecan (FOLFIRI) in the adjuvant treatment was compared with surgery\nfollowed by 5-FU/LV [14]. Thus, the importance of early cytotoxic regimen was\nsuggested to eradicate active micrometastases. Sandwiched perioperative chemotherapy is\nanother option. The EORTC 40983 trial reported the efficacy of perioperative chemotherapy\nwith FOLFOX4 and surgery compared with surgery alone, and the primary endpoint was\nPFS [15]. Patients received six cycles of FOLFOX4 chemotherapy both preoperatively and\npostoperatively. Suggested reasons for the lack of difference in OS include\nlimited number of enrolled patients, limitations associated with surgical techniques, and\nsubsequent chemotherapy in cases of recurrence. However, there was no significant improvement in OS\nand chemotherapy increased the post-operative complications.\nBased on the PFS benefit of the EORTC 40983 trial, the New EPOC trial assessed the\nrole of perioperative chemotherapy with or without cetuximab in resectable CLM with\nwild-type KRAS [19].     "\nPreviously, clinicopathologic factors were used to define\nthe appropriateness of attempting hepatectomy for colorectal metastases. These factors concentrated on the lesions to\nbe removed and patient factors rather than the liver that\nwould remain. In a 1986 publication by Ekberg et al. [37],\nthe authors proposed several general contraindications to\nhepatectomy for metastatic colorectal cancer. These criteria\nincluded: four or more metastases within the liver, additional extrahepatic metastatic disease, large size of hepatic\nmetastases, and the inability to achieve a resection margin\nof at least 1 cm. Multiple subsequent studies from other\ngroups then corroborated some or all of these findings.\nWith time, these clinicopathologic factors were used to determine which patients with colorectal liver metastases had\n\u201cresectable\u201d disease.\nSubsequently, these studies were criticized both on clinical and methodological grounds. One major problem is\nthat only a small number of patients who actually met all the\n\u201cunresectable\u201d criteria were included in the analyses. Additionally, most of these studies used only univariate (logrank) analyses, which do not adjust for competing risk\nfactors that may have affected survival.
