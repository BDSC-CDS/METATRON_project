[1] Additionally, a\nsignificant number of patients with initially early-stage CRC will\nlater develop metastatic recurrence despite treatment. [2] The most\ncommon sites of metastatic disease are the liver and lungs, with\napproximately 60% of patients with liver-only metastases having\nsynchronous disease and 40% having metachronous disease. [3-8] With\nsurgical advances, the proportion of patients able to undergo liver\nresection with curative intent has increased, including some patients\ninitially presenting with unresectable disease who then respond\nwell to initial systemic therapy. [9] Patients who are not surgical\ncandidates due to comorbidities or who have liver metastases in\ndifficult anatomical locations may still be considered for curativeintent radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT). Recent data suggest that selected patients with\nliver metastases could also be treated effectively with curative-intent\nthermal ablation, [10] an option for patients not fit for resection. In this\nreport, we focus on liver resection as the primary curative management strategy, as it is considered the gold standard treatment of liver\nmetastases.\nThere is no consensus definition of what constitutes \u201cresectable\u201d\ncolorectal liver metastases, with the decision regarding the potential\nfor surgical intervention in each patient ideally made by a multidisciplinary team (MDT), including colorectal and liver surgeons,\nradiologists, nuclear medicine physicians, pathologists, radiation\noncologists, and medical oncologists. The decision for surgery incorporates review of the cancer history, the current imaging, and the\npatient\u2019s fitness for a major surgical intervention. To our knowledge\nthere is no large, multicentre, prospective series documenting initial\nassessment of resectability that also captures detailed patient, disease,\ntreatment and outcome data, including the use of initial systemic\ntherapy and/or liver resection.\nHere we reviewed the outcomes of metastatic CRC (mCRC)\npatients with liver-only disease, based on the prospectively\ndocumented initial assessments of resectability in a real-world\nAustralian population. Each patient, following the initial diagnosis\nof metastatic disease, was classified as having resectable, potentially\nresectable (pending a good response to initial systemic therapy),\nor unresectable disease by the treating clinician in consultation\nwith MDT review. We assessed the correlation of the initial\nassessment with the clinical outcome, identified factors associated\n\nwith outcomes, and considered the broader implications of these\nfindings.\n\n_"
