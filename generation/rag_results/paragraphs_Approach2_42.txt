    "\nApproximately 20% to 25% of colorectal cancer (CRC) patients\nare diagnosed with de novo metastatic disease. [1] Additionally, a\nsignificant number of patients with initially early-stage CRC will\nlater develop metastatic recurrence despite treatment. [2] The most\ncommon sites of metastatic disease are the liver and lungs, with\napproximately 60% of patients with liver-only metastases having\nsynchronous disease and 40% having metachronous disease. [3-8] With\nsurgical advances, the proportion of patients able to undergo liver\nresection with curative intent has increased, including some patients\ninitially presenting with unresectable disease who then respond\nwell to initial systemic therapy. [9] Patients who are not surgical\ncandidates due to comorbidities or who have liver metastases in\ndifficult anatomical locations may still be considered for curativeintent radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT). Recent data suggest that selected patients with\nliver metastases could also be treated effectively with curative-intent\nthermal ablation, [10] an option for patients not fit for resection. In this\nreport, we focus on liver resection as the primary curative management strategy, as it is considered the gold standard treatment of liver\nmetastases.\nThere is no consensus definition of what constitutes \u201cresectable\u201d\ncolorectal liver metastases, with the decision regarding the potential\nfor surgical intervention in each patient ideally made by a multidisciplinary team (MDT), including colorectal and liver surgeons,\nradiologists, nuclear medicine physicians, pathologists, radiation\noncologists, and medical oncologists. The decision for surgery incorporates review of the cancer history, the current imaging, and the\npatient\u2019s fitness for a major surgical intervention.
